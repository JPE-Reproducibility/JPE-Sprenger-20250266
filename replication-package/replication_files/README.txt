OVERVIEW
This replication package contains data and code to reproduce the tables and figures in "Connecting Common Ratio and Common Consequence Preferences." The archive includes (i) raw data downloaded from Qualtrics and Prolific (anonymized), (ii) cleaned/analysis datasets produced by the provided Stata scripts, and (iii) analysis code in Stata, R, and Matlab that generates the paper's tables and figures.

Recommended run order (high level):
1. Update directory paths in the main scripts (Stata/R/Matlab).
2. Run Stata to clean data and generate most tables/figures and in-text statistics.
3. Run Matlab to generate structural estimation outputs and figures.
4. Run R to generate Appendix D tables/figures (MLE).

Approximate runtimes on the listed machine:
- Stata cleaning + outputs: ~20 seconds
- Matlab scripts: ~10 seconds
- R (MLE pipeline): ~100 minutes

SUPPORTING MATERIALS: SURVEY INSTRUMENTS AND EXPERIMENTAL INSTRUCTIONS

The entirety of the following information is included here in the README, and the underlying materials are included in the folder “experimental-materials/”.

1. The subject pool and recruiting procedures.
We conducted the experiment on Prolific.co. We recruited 2,102 participants through Prolific who met the following eligibility criteria: at least a high school education; between the ages of 18 and 31; living in the United States or Western Europe; minimum Prolific approval rating of 99 percent; fluent in English; and 50 to 1,000 previous Prolific submissions. We also recruited a gender-balanced sample with an even split of male and female participants.

2. The experimental technology—when and where the experiments were conducted; by computer or manually; online, and so forth.
We conducted the experiment online on October 5, 2022. We recruited subjects through the Prolific research platform. The experiment was programmed using Qualtrics.

3. Any procedures to test for comprehension before running the experiment, including the use of practice trials and quizzes.
Before stage 1, participants complete an unincentivized attention check and a quiz about the payment mechanism. After completing stage 2, they complete two incentivized comprehension checks to gauge their understanding of the multiple price list format and the binary choice tasks. The first tests whether individuals can correctly fill out a price list given a specified indifference value. The second tests whether participants can correctly answer a binary choice question when given another person's responses to a multiple price list. We did not exclude subjects from the experiment based on these questions.

4. Matching procedures, especially for game theory experiments.
The experiment is an individual decision-making study, so there was no matching between participants.

5. Subject payments, including whether artificial currency was used, the exchange rate, show-up fees, average earnings, lotteries, and/or grades.
We paid each participant a fixed $5 USD fee for completing the experiment. In addition, we randomly selected one in five participants to receive a bonus based on their responses. Specifically, we randomly chose one of their 46 decisions (20 valuations, 24 choices, and two incentivized comprehension checks) to be the decision that counts. If the decision that counts was a valuation task, then we randomly selected one row of the price list and paid the participant according to their choice in that row. If the decision that counts was a binary choice task, then we paid the participant based on the option they selected. If the decision that counts was a comprehension-check question, then we paid the participant $5 if they answered correctly. The average bonus payment for selected participants was $15.76. All payments were described in USD throughout the study.

6. The number of subjects used in each session and, where relevant, their experience.
We conducted a single session with 2,102 participants. Subjects had no relevant prior experience; subjects who had participated in a pilot study were excluded from participating in the main study.

7. Timing, such as how long a typical session lasted, and how much of that time was instructional.
Subjects read through the instructions at their own pace. The average completion time for the entire study was 24 minutes and 27 seconds.

8. Any use of deception and/or any instructional inaccuracies.
We did not use any deception.

9. Detailed statement of protocols.
The study consists of two stages. In stage 1, subjects completed 20 valuation tasks providing their valuation for a given lottery. In stage 2, subjects completed 24 binary choice tasks providing a choice between two lotteries. Tasks varied in terms of their parameter values (referenced as p,r in the text) as well as their question-type (referenced as AB, AB', CD in the text). Stage 2 choices also systematically varied the prizes associated with each lottery. For a given (p,r), subjects complete all three question types for both valuations and choices. Tasks were linked across stages such that a given parameter constellation and question type for which a subject completed a stage 1 valuation would also be completed as a stage 2 binary choice. After every fifth task (in both stages), subjects were provided a break exercise asking them to spot a camouflaged animal.

10. Samples of permission forms and record sheets.
N/A. We recorded all data through Qualtrics, and recorded participant information through Prolific.

11. Copies of instructions and slides/transparencies used to present instructions.
We provide the full set of instructions as screenshots.

12. Source code for computer programs used to conduct the experiment and to analyze the data.
We provide the Qualtrics .qsf file (experimental program) and all analysis code in this replication package.

13. Screen shots showing how the programs are used.
We provide a full set of screenshots for the program.

DESCRIPTION OF PROGRAMS/CODE

High-level structure
- stata/: Stata scripts for data cleaning, most tables/figures, and in-text statistics.
- R/: R scripts for Appendix D MLE estimation and related tables/figures.
- matlab/: Matlab scripts for structural estimates and selected figures/tables.
- tables/ and figures/: output folders filled by scripts.

Stata programs (stata/)
- mainS.do
  Runs the full Stata workflow to (a) clean the data, (b) generate tables and figures (except those listed below
  under R/Matlab), and (c) produce statistics reported in the text.

- clean_data.do
  Imports and merges/cleans raw data files to produce final analysis datasets:
  ExperimentData, demographics, part1_cleaned, part2_cleaned.
  Outputs are written to cleaned-data/ in both .dta and .csv formats.
  Variable definitions [dataset]_definitions.csv are generated automatically.

- makeTable2.do
  Produces Table 2 from the main text using ExperimentData.dta. Requires estout.

- makeFigures3-7.do
  Produces Figures 3–7 from the main text using:
  ExperimentData.dta, demographics.dta, part1_cleaned.dta, part2_cleaned.dta. Requires binscatter.

- makeAppendixTables.do
  Produces Tables A.1–A.9 and C.1 using part1_cleaned.dta and part2_cleaned.dta.
  Requires unique and estout.

- makeAppendixFigures.do
  Produces Appendix figures using part1_cleaned.dta and part2_cleaned.dta.
  Requires unique and estout.

- statsInText.do
  Produces additional statistics reported in the main text using:
  part1_cleaned.dta, part2_cleaned.dta, demographics.dta, ExperimentData.dta.

Matlab programs (matlab/)
- structuralEstimates.m
  Uses part1_cleaned.csv to produce Figures 8, A.5, F.1–F.3, G.1–G.6 and Tables F.1 and G.2.
  Supporting custom functions are in matlab/functions/.

- makeFigure1.m
  Uses ExperimentData.csv to produce Figure 1 (main text).

- makeFigure9.m
  Produces Figure 9 (main text). No data used.

- makeFigureE1.m
  Produces Figure E.1 (Appendix). No data used.

R programs (R/)
- mainR.R
  Runs the full R workflow: imports/prepares data (using part2_cleaned.csv), estimates the MLE model,
  and produces Appendix D tables/figures.

- reducedForm.R
  Implements MLE estimation from Appendix D.

- functions.R
  Supporting functions.

- makeFigureD1.R
  Produces Figure D1.

- makeTableD1.R
  Produces Table D1.

- makeTableD2.R
  Produces Table D2.

Outputs generated directly in LaTeX (no data)
- Tables 1 and 3, and Figure 2 do not use any data and are generated directly in the LaTeX document.


INSTRUCTIONS TO REPLICATORS

Before you run anything
1. Update directories/paths in:
   - stata/mainS.do
   - R/mainR.R
   - matlab/structuralEstimates.m
   - matlab/makeFigure1.m
   - matlab/makeFigure9.m
   - matlab/makeFigureE1.m

Replication steps
1. Run Stata: stata/mainS.do
   - This calls clean_data.do, which cleans raw data and saves cleaned datasets in cleaned-data/.
   - Generates Stata produced tables/figures and in-text stats.

2. Run Matlab (order not important):
   - matlab/structuralEstimates.m
   - matlab/makeFigure1.m
   - matlab/makeFigure9.m
   - matlab/makeFigureE1.m

3. Run R: R/mainR.R
   - Produces Appendix D tables/figures.

All output is stored in the tables/ and figures/ folders.
